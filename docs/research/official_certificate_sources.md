# Schobot Official Certificate Sources — Maharashtra

**Research checked:** 25 November 2026 (IST)  
**Scope:** Publicly accessible Maharashtra and Government of India sources for an eventual certificate-document OCR/RAG corpus. This is source research, not legal advice and not an implementation specification.

## Verification approach

Only government portals and documents hosted or published by government bodies are treated as verified sources. Search-engine summaries and third-party explainers were not used as evidence. A link is marked **verified** only where the government page or document was opened and its relevant contents inspected. Where a portal exposes a service but not its post-login application steps, that limitation is stated rather than filled in by assumption.

The public Aaple Sarkar pages do not show a publication or last-updated date. Their contents were checked on the date above. The linked application forms have PDF metadata dates noted below; Mumbai City Collectorate pages list the forms as dated 27 January 2021.

## 1. Income Certificate

### Official service and authority

- **Portal service name:** Income Certificate (the Aaple Sarkar service table has the typographical rendering “lncome Certificate”).
- **Department:** Government of Maharashtra, Revenue and Forest Department, Revenue sub-department.
- **Designated officer:** Nayab Tahsildar.
- **Notified service time:** 15 days. First appeal: Tahsildar. Second appeal: Sub-Divisional Officer.
- **Official online portal:** Aaple Sarkar; the service directory marks it as available online.

The publicly opened service entry gives the authority and service timeline. It does not expose a complete, unauthenticated, step-by-step application walkthrough. The safe procedural statement for a future knowledge base is: use the Income Certificate service listed on Aaple Sarkar and follow the current on-screen instructions. Do not assert an unverified fee, payment sequence, or offline submission route.

### Supporting documents in the official application form

The Revenue Department form asks for applicant and family details, occupation, annual income by source, agricultural land details, beneficiary, and the purpose for which the certificate is requested. Its attachment checklist identifies:

- Applicant photograph.
- Identity proof, with examples including voter ID, passport, driving licence, RSBY card, government/semi-government identity card, PAN, MNREGA job card, or Aadhaar.
- Address proof, with examples including electoral-roll extract, water or electricity bill, telephone bill, ration card, rent receipt, land extracts, property-tax/registration records, Aadhaar, passport, or driving licence.
- Age proof, with examples including school admission/bonafide/leaving records, birth certificate, or government/semi-government service book.
- Income evidence matched to the income source. The form gives examples such as 7/12 and 8-A land extracts plus Talathi report for a landowner; Form 16 and a Circle Officer verification report for salary income; a bank certificate for a pensioner; and an income-tax return.
- A medical/health-authority certificate is listed as an additional document when the application is for medical facilities.

These are examples and source-specific evidence, not a claim that every applicant must submit every listed item. The form requests an income certificate for a one-year or three-year period; this wording should not be converted into a general validity rule without a separate current authority source.

### Eligibility and rules

The opened official form documents income sources and asks for the purpose of the certificate, but does **not** state a universal income ceiling for obtaining an Income Certificate. A scholarship or benefit's income threshold is a separate scheme rule; it must not be presented as a certificate-issuance condition.

The form header cites a 2012 Maharashtra Government Resolution reference: *Sankirna 1/2012/Pr.Kr.18/E-1, Appendix B, Form G* (transliterated from the Marathi header). The standalone GR was not located and verified in this research. Treat the form as a verified application source, not as a substitute for the GR or current legal guidance.

### Language and downloadable material

The Aaple Sarkar service entry is available in English and Marathi. The government-hosted application form inspected is predominantly Marathi. The Mumbai City Collectorate English webpage lists the same named application form with a date of 27 January 2021. PDF metadata records creation on 15 April 2014 and modification on 16 April 2014; this does not establish that no later revision exists.

**Initial RAG suitability:** Good for the application fields and document checklist, provided the Marathi form is OCR'd with a Marathi-capable pipeline, source/version metadata is retained, and any translation is reviewed. Not sufficient by itself to answer scheme-specific income eligibility or current certificate validity.

## 2. Caste Certificate

### Official service and authority

- **Portal service name:** Caste Certificate.
- **Department:** Government of Maharashtra, Revenue and Forest Department, Revenue sub-department.
- **Designated officer:** Sub-Divisional Officer / Deputy Collector.
- **Notified service time:** 45 days. First appeal: Additional Collector. Second appeal: Collector.
- **Official online portal:** Aaple Sarkar; the official service directory marks the service as available online.

The separate service titled **Caste Certificate Verification Committee** appears in the official services directory under the Social Justice and Special Assistance Department with a separate 90-day service time. A caste certificate and a caste-validity decision are therefore distinct services. The caste service's attachment menu also lists “Caste Validity” as one possible supporting document; that does not make caste validity interchangeable with the caste certificate.

### Supporting documents in the official Aaple Sarkar entry

The opened Caste Certificate service page groups documents as:

- **Proof of Identity:** examples include PAN, passport, RSBY card, MNREGA job card, driving licence, applicant photo, and government/semi-government identity card.
- **Proof of Address:** examples include passport, water bill, ration card, Aadhaar, voter ID, telephone/electricity bills, driving licence, property-tax receipt, and 7/12/8-A extracts or rent receipt.
- **Other Documents:** a long menu of possible evidence, including applicant/father/relative caste certificates, school leaving certificates of applicant or relatives, caste-validity documents of relatives, land and rights records, Kotwal-book extracts, birth/death register extracts, Talathi or local enquiry reports, affidavits, gazette name-change evidence, and residence records.

The portal labels these categories “Any -1” in English and “किमान -1” in Marathi. The page does not clearly explain whether that selection rule applies uniformly to every caste/community case. The list is broad and includes alternatives; it must not be represented as a requirement to submit every listed item. The evidence needed can depend on the applicant's case and record history.

### Eligibility and applicable Maharashtra rules

The public checklist confirms that this is a caste-certificate service and identifies caste-linked evidence, but it does not provide a complete eligibility test, an authoritative current list of communities, or the rules for migrants and ancestry claims. Those points need the current Maharashtra statute, rules, Government Resolutions, and applicable community schedules before a RAG system answers them. No standalone caste-issuance Act/Rules/GR document was successfully opened and verified in this research.

### Language and downloadable material

The Aaple Sarkar service detail is published under both English and Marathi routes. The two opened pages carry mostly English document names, with Marathi headings/labels on the Marathi route. No separate official downloadable caste application/guideline PDF was verified; the public service-detail webpage is the verified checklist source.

**Initial RAG suitability:** Useful as an official checklist and service-authority reference, but not sufficient for legal eligibility answers. Preserve the document groups as alternatives, and route legal category, migration, and validity questions to verified legal sources or human review.

## 3. Domicile Certificate

### Official service and authority

- **Official Maharashtra service name:** **Age, Nationality and Domicile Certificate** (combined service; the opened Aaple Sarkar directory does not list a separate Domicile Certificate service).
- **Department:** Government of Maharashtra, Revenue and Forest Department, Revenue sub-department.
- **Designated officer:** Tahsildar.
- **Notified service time:** 15 days. First appeal: Sub-Divisional Officer. Second appeal: Additional Collector.
- **Official online portal:** Aaple Sarkar; the official directory marks the service as available online.

### Supporting documents and information in the official form

The application form collects current and permanent addresses, the year residence began, Maharashtra residence duration, migration history, education, property ownership/relationship to property, and (where applicable) marriage and spouse-residence details. Its attachment checklist identifies:

- Applicant photograph.
- Identity proof.
- Address proof.
- Age proof.
- Residence proof, with examples of certificates from the Talathi, Gramsevak, or Bill Collector.
- Additional property-ownership evidence where applicable, such as a property-tax receipt, 7/12 and 8-A extracts, rent receipt, utility bill, ration card, electoral-roll extract, or property-registration extract.
- Marriage certificate and spouse-residence proof for the form's married-woman case.

The form's residence history questions are useful evidence for a future checklist, but the form itself does not establish a universal minimum residence period. Do not encode a commonly repeated number of years as a Maharashtra eligibility rule unless the current applicable Government Resolution or other controlling official source is separately verified.

### Applicable guidance, language, and dates

The form header cites *Sankirna 1/2012/Pr.Kr.18/E-1, Appendix B, Form T* (transliterated from its Marathi header). The standalone GR was not located and verified. The direct government-hosted PDF has PDF metadata creation on 15 April 2014 and modification on 16 April 2014. The Mumbai City Collectorate's English listing dates its application form 27 January 2021. The form inspected is predominantly Marathi; the Aaple Sarkar service page is available in English and Marathi.

**Initial RAG suitability:** Good for form fields and a conditional document checklist, with Marathi OCR and human review. Not enough to answer a statutory residence-duration question.

## 4. Nationality Certificate

### Maharashtra service found

The Maharashtra public service identified in this research is **Age, Nationality and Domicile Certificate**, issued by the Tahsildar through the Revenue Department. The official form is a single combined application and includes the same identity, address, age, and residence evidence described in the domicile section. No separate Maharashtra Revenue Department service titled only “Nationality Certificate” was found in the official service directory checked.

For Schobot's document taxonomy, record this as a **combined Maharashtra certificate service** and do not assume there is a distinct state nationality-only form or separate list of supporting documents. The combined form asks for residence and family information but does not state a complete legal test for Indian citizenship/nationality.

### Distinction from central Indian citizenship services

The Government of India's Indian Citizenship Online portal describes acquisition of citizenship by birth, descent, registration, and naturalisation under the Citizenship Act, 1955. It provides online application routes for statutory registration/naturalisation categories and describes application scrutiny, document verification, and (for relevant routes) an oath process. Its CAA-specific section has its own eligibility limits and should not be generalized to all applicants.

This central citizenship process is **not the same thing** as the Maharashtra Age, Nationality and Domicile Certificate service. The central portal is appropriate only when the question concerns a formal citizenship application under the relevant central law; it is not evidence for the Maharashtra form's nationality-document checklist.

### Language and sufficiency

The Maharashtra form is predominantly Marathi, while the service page is available in English and Marathi. The central citizenship portal inspected is in English. A dedicated Maharashtra nationality-only guideline or a standalone legal criteria document was not verified. This category therefore needs clarification and further research before it is represented as an independent certificate type in a RAG system.

## 5. Common official guidelines

1. **Use the live Aaple Sarkar service entry as the operational source.** The Government of Maharashtra's notified-services directory provides the service names, designated officers, appeal officers, service times, and whether a service is available online. For the entries checked, the notified periods are 15 days for Income, 45 days for Caste, and 15 days for the combined Age, Nationality and Domicile service. These are service-time entries, not guarantees about an individual case.
2. **Do not collapse related certificates.** Caste Certificate, Caste Certificate Verification Committee/caste validity, and Income Certificate are separate services. Maharashtra's combined Age, Nationality and Domicile service should also remain distinct from central citizenship registration/naturalisation.
3. **Treat checklists as conditional.** The official forms provide alternatives and case-specific examples. A document mentioned in a checklist is not automatically mandatory for every applicant.
4. **Separate certificate evidence from scheme eligibility.** An income limit, caste category, domicile condition, or nationality condition required by a scholarship is not necessarily a rule for issuing the certificate itself.
5. **Keep the underlying GR/legal texts as a gap.** The income and combined age/nationality/domicile PDFs cite a 2012 Maharashtra GR reference. The full standalone GR was not verified here. The caste-specific legal framework and community schedules also remain to be collected from official sources.
6. **Preserve language and provenance.** Keep the original Marathi document, English portal page, exact URL, retrieval date, PDF metadata, page number, and OCR confidence. Any English translation should be marked as a translation and reviewed by a competent Marathi reader.

## 7. Missing documents and research gaps

- The standalone 2012 Maharashtra Government Resolution cited in the Income and combined Age/Nationality/Domicile form headers was not located and verified. The exact reference is transcribed from the form header; confirm its Marathi text and retrieve the authoritative GR before treating it as controlling policy.
- No complete current Maharashtra caste-issuance Act/Rules/GR, current community schedule, migration guidance, or official caste-validity guideline was opened. The Aaple Sarkar checklist alone is not sufficient for eligibility answers.
- The Income service's public detail page does not expose the attachment checklist; that checklist came from the Revenue PDF. Confirm the online service's current requirements because portal checklists can change independently of older form PDFs.
- No current official document establishing a universal Maharashtra domicile-residence minimum was found. The application form asks for residence history but does not itself state a minimum.
- No separate Maharashtra nationality-only service or document was found. The verified state service is bundled with age and domicile; the central portal is for citizenship processes under central law.
- No current certificate specimen, QR-verification specification, cryptographic signature guidance, or official certificate-field schema was verified. Do not assume a certificate template from an application form.
- The Marathi PDFs' extracted text contains font/glyph artefacts. A Marathi-capable OCR workflow and human proofreading against the original pages are prerequisites for any reliable English-language knowledge base.
- The citizenship portal was inspected at the homepage level; its linked orders and form PDFs need item-by-item version and content verification before ingestion.

## 8. Recommendations for future document ingestion

1. Keep separate collections or strong jurisdiction/type metadata for: Maharashtra Income Certificate; Maharashtra Caste Certificate; caste validity; the combined Maharashtra Age/Nationality/Domicile service; and central citizenship registration/naturalisation.
2. For every captured source, preserve the exact title, issuing authority, source URL, access date, page/section, language, PDF metadata dates, and a content hash. Re-check undated portal pages periodically.
3. Store the original Marathi PDF alongside OCR output. Preserve page-level provenance and OCR confidence; do not silently substitute a machine translation for the source.
4. Represent document lists as conditional alternatives with their original grouping and notes. Never flatten “examples,” “any one,” “if applicable,” and mandatory items into a single universal checklist.
5. Keep procedural facts, certificate-issuance rules, scheme eligibility rules, and central citizenship law in separate retrieval categories. Require the answer to name the jurisdiction and certificate service it is discussing.
6. Add the missing primary legal sources (especially the cited GR and caste law/rules) before answering legal eligibility questions. Until then, respond that the official legal criteria were not verified and direct users to the competent authority.
7. Prefer the current Aaple Sarkar service-detail page for officer/timeline metadata and the government-hosted form for its checklist. Do not treat the portal's processing time as a guarantee or its broad document menu as an exhaustive mandatory list.

### Sufficiency conclusion

- **Income Certificate:** Sufficient official references for an initial procedural/document-checklist corpus (Aaple Sarkar service metadata plus the Maharashtra Revenue form). The underlying cited GR and current online checklist still need verification.
- **Caste Certificate:** Partially covered. The official service authority, timeline, and broad evidence menu are verified, but legal eligibility, current community schedules, migration rules, and caste-validity policy still need primary-source research.
- **Domicile Certificate:** Partially covered for the combined service, application fields, and evidence checklist. The controlling GR and any minimum residence rule remain unverified.
- **Nationality Certificate:** Not sufficiently established as a standalone Maharashtra certificate category. The verified state service is combined with age and domicile; central citizenship registration/naturalisation is a different legal process.

**Overall:** There are verified official sources to seed a carefully scoped document checklist for all four labels, but none should yet be used as a complete legal eligibility authority. Income is the strongest preliminary category; Caste, Domicile, and especially standalone Nationality require additional official legal-source research.
